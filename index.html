The Ladder

DBR is a doubles-adjusted Elo. Each season opens with everyone on their skill index × 100, so the admin's ratings set the starting order and results take over from there; a pair's strength is the average of its two players, the winning margin scales the swing, and underdog credit hands more of a win to the lower-rated partner. Under 6 matches a rating is provisional.

Rating rules

New players move at 1.6× this until they clear the provisional mark. Higher K means the ladder reacts faster and jumps around more.

At zero both partners take an identical swing. Turn it up and the lower-rated partner banks more of a win while the favourite carries more of a loss — which is what stops a strong player farming rating off a weak one.

11–2 says more than 11–9, and it is damped so a heavy favourite gains little from a thrashing. The margin is read as a share of the winning score, so 11–4 and 7–2 count the same — you can mix full and short games in one league without skewing anyone's rating.

Match defaults

At zero there is no ceiling: a brand-new player who beats a far stronger pair 11–0 can jump well over a hundred points in one game. Set a number here if that ever feels too wild.

Cards already filed keep the numbers they were given — changing the scale does not rescale them, so change it before you start rating, not halfway through.

How the two ratings differ

DBR is earned. It moves only when you win or lose, it cannot be talked up, and it is the ladder. The skill index is judged — spiking, serving, setting, receiving and placement, each out of eleven, filed by the club admin alone. They disagree on purpose: a player with a big spike and no receive sits high on skill and low on the ladder, and that gap is the useful bit — it tells you what to work on.
